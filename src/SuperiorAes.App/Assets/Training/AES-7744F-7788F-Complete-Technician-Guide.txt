1. Scope, safety and how to use this guide
This manual is a field reference for AES IntelliNet 7744F and 7788F legacy subscriber units. It covers the AES 7041 handheld
programmer key functions and their PC/Tera Term equivalents. The programming language is similar on both models, but the
physical behavior of several zone inputs differs.
Life-safety warning
Before powering, resetting, programming, keying or testing an installed subscriber, notify the central station and place the
account on test. A subscriber can transmit immediately after power-up or reset. Programming must conform to the
approved system design, current code requirements, the authority having jurisdiction and the central station's AES
network requirements.
What this guide includes
- Verified US232R and DB9-breakout wiring to radio connector J1.
 
- Complete handheld-to-PC command conversion.
 
- Every documented programming, monitor and diagnostic command for these models.
 
- Timer ranges, defaults and the practical effect of each timer.
 
- 7788F EOL-zone behavior and 7744F EOL/reversing-voltage differences.
 
- Status, self-test codes, monitor-packet fields, routing entries and signal-quality interpretation.
 
- A full commissioning, troubleshooting and closeout sequence.

J1 pin 6 is always the dangerous conductor
Physical J1 pin 6 is approximately +12 V from the radio. In Case A it is WHITE; in the reversed Case B it is BLUE. Insulate

readable SETUP UNIT and ENTER ID prompts with standard polarity.

Data-entry rules
- Use upper-case A-F when entering hexadecimal IDs or cipher values.
 
- At most programming prompts, pressing Enter without entering a new value preserves the old value.
 
- The subscriber normally displays OLD and NEW fields. Verify the old value before changing it.
 
- Programming functions normally time out after approximately 65 seconds.
 
- If Tera Term appears not to show what you type, remember that local echo is normally off; watch for the subscriber response.
 
- Use the physical Reset button after zone-programming changes and after completing monitor work.
 
F1 through F3
The AES manuals provide the PC equivalents Ctrl+Q, Ctrl+R and Ctrl+S but do not assign separate standalone field
functions to handheld F1, F2 or F3 for the 7744F/7788F. Do not invent a function based on another AES model.

4. f - Program subscriber ID and cipher
SETUP UNIT-OLD: NEW
ENTER ID#-1234:....
CPHR CODE-XXXX:....
OK
Subscriber ID
The ID is the subscriber's unique four-digit hexadecimal network address. Valid characters are 0-9 and upper-case A-F.
Examples include 4923, 12AF and B804. Every subscriber on the AES network must have a unique ID.
- Entering a new value overwrites the stored ID.
 
- Pressing Enter at a blank ID prompt preserves the current ID.
 
- A reused ID can send signals under the wrong account and create serious dispatch or reporting problems.
 
System cipher
The cipher is a four-digit hexadecimal network security code assigned by the AES network administrator. It must match the
cipher used by the central receiver or the IP-Link through which the subscriber communicates.
- The old cipher is intentionally displayed as XXXX.
 
- Pressing Enter without a new cipher preserves the stored cipher.
 
- A wrong cipher prevents normal network enrollment and communication.
 
Symptoms of an incorrect cipher or invalid network identity
- WA indicator continues flashing.
 
- The subscriber does not receive packet acknowledgements.
 
- Local status may show RT1:XXXX or LEVEL:255.
 
- The radio may transmit, but the unit never becomes a healthy network participant.
 
Local-only programming
ID and cipher can only be changed with a programmer or PC terminal attached directly to J1. Remote programming of
these two values is not permitted. A RAM reset does not erase them.

Check-in interval
CHKIN TIME--OLD:NEW
ENTER HRS----24:..
ENTER MINS---00:..
A check-in is a periodic supervisory data message confirming that the subscriber is alive and participating in the network. It is not
an alarm. Shorter intervals permit quicker recognition of a missing subscriber but increase RF and central-station traffic.
- A 24-hour interval is typical except in higher-security applications.
 
- 00 hours, 00 minutes is illegal.
 
- Do not program a total interval greater than 24 hours.
 
- The central automation missing-check-in threshold must agree with the programmed interval.
 
Manual range wording
The display documents hours as 00-24, while the explanatory text says 1-24 and separately states that 00:00 is illegal.
This indicates zero hours can be used with a nonzero minute value on compatible firmware. Use only the interval
approved for the network and installation.
AC report delay
AC RPT TIME-OLD:NEW [RM] or [00-60]
ENTER MINS---RM:..
This value is applied after the subscriber recognizes an AC failure or AC restore. The subscriber requires approximately 100
minutes of continuous AC loss to recognize a failure, and approximately 100 minutes of restored AC to recognize a restore.
 Setting
 Effect
RM
Random additional delay from 0 to 60 minutes; factory default
00
No additional delay after the approximately 100-minute detection period
01-60
Fixed additional delay in minutes
With RM, an AC event is normally reported approximately 100-160 minutes after the physical condition begins. Random delay
prevents large numbers of subscribers from transmitting simultaneously during an area-wide outage.

5. Timing parameters - continued
NTR report delay
NTR RPT DLY-NNN:... [0-330 seconds]
This is the minimum spacing applied to the subscriber's own reports. The default and recommended setting is 10 seconds. It
meters multiple events so the subscriber does not continuously occupy the network.
- Range: 0 to 330 seconds.
 
- Default: 10 seconds.
 
- Zero removes the spacing delay and is not recommended.
 
- Commercial fire and applicable UL burglary installations require no more than 20 seconds.
 
- If the previous delay has already expired, a newly generated event can begin transmission immediately.
 
Delay versus suppression
AC report delay determines when an AC event is transmitted. AC suppression in the i menu prevents the event from being
transmitted at all. Do not confuse these settings.
Timing examples
 Example
 What happens
Check-in 24:00
The subscriber creates a check-in about once every 24 hours.
AC delay RM
After about 100 minutes of AC loss, an additional random 0-60 minute delay is selected.
AC delay 00
After about 100 minutes of AC loss, the AC-fail message is ready without an added delay.
NTR delay 10
The subscriber normally spaces its own queued reports by at least 10 seconds.
AC suppressed Y
No AC-fail report is sent, regardless of the AC-delay value.

6. h - Zone programming
Zone programming controls Fire/Trouble packet reporting, the electrical interpretation of every zone and whether each zone
reports a return to normal.
Reset required after zone changes
After completing zone programming, press the physical Reset button. The reset initializes the inputs under their new
configuration and clears former conditions. Without it, an input changed to Bypass or another type can continue to show a
confusing old alarm or trouble state.
Fire/Trouble packet prompt
FIRE/
TROUBLE PKT-N: . [Y/N]
 Selection
 Behavior
Y
Transmit zone-trouble messages. Required for applicable fire/UL installations. Disables normal remote programming.
N
Do not transmit zone-trouble messages. Not appropriate for required fire trouble reporting.
When Fire/Trouble packets are enabled, normal central-station remote programming is disabled. Completing f, g or h through a
locally connected programmer can temporarily open an approximately 10-minute remote-programming window. The window
begins after the local function is completed or after its 65-second timeout.
Zone type entry
ZONE BANK 0
SET ZONE (BSF) B0
OLD SSSSSSSS LOW>HI
NEW ........
Enter exactly eight letters, in zone order from 1 through 8. Entering more or fewer than eight values causes the prompt to repeat.
 Code
 Name
 EOL-zone behavior
B
Bypassed
Input is ignored; no normal zone alarm/trouble processing
S
Supervised
Normal at approximately 2.2 kOhm; alarm on open or short
F
Fire supervised
Normal at approximately 2.2 kOhm; trouble on open; alarm on short
Why S and F are different
Supervised S identifies both an open and short as an alarm-type abnormal condition. Fire-supervised F distinguishes a
broken/open circuit as trouble and a short circuit as alarm. This distinction is normally required for fire-service inputs.

7. i - Operating modes
SET MODES--OLD:NEW
ENABLE RPTNG-Y: .
SPRSS ACFAIL-N: .
SPRSS CHRGFLT-N: .
SPRSS GNDFLT-N: .
Enable repeating
 Value
 Behavior
Y
Subscriber may receive and relay packets for other AES units. Factory default.
N
Subscriber does not serve as a packet repeater for other units.
Repeating is a core mesh-network function. Disabling it may not prevent the subscriber from reporting its own events, but it
removes the unit as a possible route for neighboring subscribers and can weaken the surrounding network. Leave repeating
enabled unless the AES network administrator specifically directs otherwise.
Suppress AC failure
Y prevents AC-failure reports from being transmitted. N permits normal AC-failure reporting and is the factory default. UL
installations require N. Suppression does not change AC detection; it hides the report.
Suppress charger fault
Y prevents battery-charger fault reports from being transmitted. N permits reporting and is the factory default. A charger fault can
indicate that the subscriber is unable to maintain its standby battery. UL installations require N.
Suppress ground fault
Y prevents ground-fault reports from being transmitted. N permits reporting and is the factory default. Suppression does not
correct an unintended connection to chassis/earth ground. UL installations require N.
Recommended normal mode values
Repeating Y; suppress AC fail N; suppress charger fault N; suppress ground fault N. Any deviation should be documented
and approved for the particular installation.

10. Monitor and traffic diagnostics
a - RX Monitor
RX Monitor displays decoded network packets addressed to this subscriber. It proves that the subscriber is receiving and
decoding traffic intended for it. It does not display every carrier or every packet exchanged by other subscribers.
b - TX Monitor
TX Monitor displays decoded protocol messages transmitted by this subscriber, including check-ins, alarms, troubles, text
messages, retries and network-management packets.
c - Monitor All
Monitor All expands receive monitoring to decoded AES network traffic within RF range. RX Monitor must already be enabled.
a   -> RX MONITOR ON
c   -> MONITOR ALL ON
b   -> TX MONITOR ON (optional, adds local transmissions)
Monitor All does not display every signal that opens squelch. The RX LED can illuminate for interference, another protocol or an
undecodable signal without producing a terminal line.

Why Monitor All can show ON but remain blank
- RX Monitor a was not enabled before Monitor All c.
 
- No AES packets are currently being transmitted within usable range.
 
- The antenna, coax or radio receiver path is defective.
 
- The received signal is interference or a different protocol.
 
- Traffic is too weak or corrupted to decode.
 
- The radio frequency/channel does not match the surrounding AES network.
 
Monitor cautions
- a, b and c are toggles; pressing the same letter again turns that mode off.
 
- Incoming text messages cannot be received while any monitor function is active.
 
- Turn all monitor functions off when finished, or press the physical Reset button.
 
Recommended monitor sequence
Reset the subscriber, wait for enrollment, press a, then c, then b if local transmit traffic is also required. Record several
complete packet/acknowledgement pairs before judging the route.

11. e - Key transmitter
KEYING TX..
TIMEOUT
The transmitter is keyed for approximately five seconds and then shuts off automatically. Press Enter during the test to abort
early; OK is displayed after an early abort. TIMEOUT at the end of the full interval is normal.
Appropriate uses
- Measure transmitter output power with an RF wattmeter.
 
- Measure forward and reflected power or antenna-system SWR.
 
- Check coax, connectors, antenna tuning and transmit current draw.
 
- Confirm that the radio and TX indicator key.
 
What the key test does not prove
- Correct packet modulation.
 
- Correct subscriber ID or cipher.
 
- Network enrollment.
 
- Reception or acknowledgement by another subscriber.
 
- Delivery of an event to the central station.
 
Because this function primarily keys the carrier for RF measurements, it may not create a normal decoded TX Monitor line.
Ctrl+Z - Display Zone Status
Zx12,Z1-8:0100-1000
The hexadecimal value is a bit pattern representing abnormal zone-input conditions. The character string gives the state of
zones 1 through 8 from left to right, with a hyphen between zones 4 and 5.
 Character
 Meaning
0
Zone is normal under its current programming
1
Faulted, alarm or otherwise not normal
T
Trouble
Use this command immediately after zone programming and reset. An unexpected 1 or T can indicate a wrong zone type,
missing 2.2 kOhm EOL resistor, open/short circuit, reversed polarity, low/no voltage on a 7744F reversing input, or stale state
because the unit was not reset after programming.

12. Ctrl+U - Text messages
ENTER MSG:
[Enter up to 200 ASCII characters, then press ENTER]
A text message can be sent from the subscriber to the central station. The subscriber exits text-entry mode if no data is entered
for approximately 65 seconds.
- Maximum message length: 200 ASCII characters.
 
- Press Enter to send.
 
- The subscriber cannot perform normal transmit or receive operation while text-entry mode is active.
 
- Incoming central-station text is displayed on the connected programmer/terminal and normally produces a beep.
 
- Incoming text cannot be received while any monitor mode is active.
 
Text message versus event test
A successful text message demonstrates useful two-way network communication, but it is not a substitute for testing
every required alarm, trouble and restoral event with the central station.
13. Troubleshooting
Readable prompts but commands do not work
- Verify flow control is None.
 
- Verify DB9 pin 7 is jumpered to pin 8 at the breakout.
 
- Recheck yellow J1 pin 2 to DB9 pin 3 TXD.
 
- Use lower-case a through j, not the handheld key combination on the PC.
 
- Ensure no other program has COM1 open.
 
Unreadable or garbled output
- Confirm 4800 baud, 8 data bits, no parity and 1 stop bit.
 
- Confirm FT_PROG inversion is OFF for every FT232R signal.
 
- Confirm blue J1 pin 1 reaches DB9 pin 5 ground.
 
- Confirm green J1 pin 3 reaches DB9 pin 2 RXD.
 
- Do not connect physical J1 pin 6: it is white in Case A and blue in reversed Case B.
 
Subscriber transmits but does not enroll
- Verify the four-digit ID is valid and unique.
 
- Verify the cipher matches the network.
 
- Inspect antenna, coax, connectors and frequency/channel.
 
- Watch WA: continuous blinking means the subscriber is not on the network.
 
- Use d; RT1:XXXX or LEVEL:255 confirms no usable network enrollment.

13. Troubleshooting - continued
Monitor All is blank
- Enable a before c.
 
- Confirm nearby network activity exists.
 
- Observe the RX LED; no RX activity suggests no usable RF energy is reaching the receiver.
 
- A steady RX LED for more than about 20 seconds can indicate interference.
 
- Keying with e is a carrier test and may not create decoded traffic.
 
Zone reads alarm or trouble unexpectedly
- Confirm the correct model: 7744F zones 5-8 are reversing-voltage inputs.
 
- Confirm the zone's B/S/F programming.
 
- Measure the EOL resistance at the subscriber terminals; account for connected-panel loading.
 
- Check for open, short, reversed polarity or low/no voltage.
 
- Press physical Reset after zone changes, then check Ctrl+Z again.
 
Acknowledgement delay
An acknowledgement delay occurs when a transmitted data packet does not receive its packet acknowledgement within the
programmed acknowledgement-delay time. The manuals identify a 60-second default acknowledgement delay. Investigate route
quality, packet retries, WA behavior, the routing table and surrounding network health.
When a persistent self-test code remains
- Press physical Reset and record the complete code.
 
- Correct power, battery, charger or ground-fault conditions first.
 
- Use RAM reset only when appropriate and after recording programming.
 
- A persistent EEPROM, ADC or loopback failure may require AES-authorized service.

Known manual typographical issues
The AES Rev 6 manuals contain a few apparent editing inconsistencies, including a 7744F zone-reset paragraph that
names the 7788F, and a testing checklist that can confuse Ctrl+T routing-table and Ctrl+Z zone-status usage. The
detailed command sections are used in this guide: Ctrl+T displays the routing table and Ctrl+Z displays zone status.
